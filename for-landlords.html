The landlord's position

You're making a £50,000
decision with a CV

A standard tenancy is a two-year commitment at £1,200 a month. That's £28,800 — before you factor in potential repair costs, void periods, and the very real chance you'll need legal action to reclaim your property if something goes wrong.

The information you're making that decision on: a credit score that doesn't measure rent, a reference written by someone who wants the tenant to leave, and a passport photo. That's the toolkit that's been available to UK landlords for 30 years.

Since May 2026, the stakes are materially higher. Section 21 is gone. If you let to the wrong person and they stop paying, you're looking at an 11-month court process, an estimated £9,000+ in losses, and a property you can't access or re-let. The asymmetry between the risk and the data available to assess it has never been greater.

Tenura exists to close that gap.

Compliance & legal

If something goes wrong,
you'll be ready

We hope you'll never need this. But if you do, having a documented, verified record of payment behaviour is the difference between a straightforward Section 8 case and a contested one.

From day one, every payment is recorded

The moment a tenancy is registered, Tenura begins logging payments — with date, amount, and verification source. Automatically, with no admin from you.

Late and missed payments are flagged immediately

You receive a notification when rent is overdue. The record is updated with the actual payment date (or flagged as missed) — creating the documentary trail a Section 8 notice requires.

A formal record is available on request

When you need to serve a Section 8 notice on grounds of rent arrears, Tenura generates a formatted payment history — timestamped, verified, and ready to present as evidence.

Your record can't be disputed

Because payment records are verified against Open Banking data, neither party can claim payments were made that weren't. The record is the record — independently verified.

The Section 8 grounds
Tenura helps you evidence

Under the Renters' Rights Act 2024, Ground 8 (mandatory) requires at least 2 months of rent arrears at the time of service and at the hearing. A Tenura record is direct evidence.

Ground 8 — mandatory
At least 2 months' rent unpaid at notice and at hearing. Tenura gives you a court-ready payment record from day one of the tenancy.
Ground 10 — discretionary
Some rent lawfully due is unpaid. A documented pattern of late payments strengthens this ground and establishes a pattern the court can assess.
Ground 11 — discretionary
Persistent rent delay even if not currently in arrears. Tenura's trend data is exactly the evidence this ground requires.

Note: this is general information, not legal advice. Always consult a qualified housing solicitor for possession proceedings.
